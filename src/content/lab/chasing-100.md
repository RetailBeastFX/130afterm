# Chasing $100

There's something I've noticed about myself when trading. Sometimes making money isn't enough. I have a number in my head, and until I reach it, it feels like I haven't accomplished anything.

November 9 was a good example.

I was up $52 by 10:48 AM. Three trades, all profitable. I could've left it alone and called it a good morning.

But I wanted $100.

I kept trading.

At some point, I got into a MU call at the wrong time. Instead of accepting that the trade wasn't working, I held onto it hoping things would turn around.

I bought the 765 call at $0.73 and eventually sold it at $0.04.

That's roughly $69 gone on one trade.

And I already knew what I was doing:

> "i got in that mu call at the wrong time and im ngl i just gambled that because i wanted to make atleast $100 today"

Looking back at the activity that day makes it even worse.

| Trading window | Result |
| -------------- | ------ |
| 9:42–10:48 AM  | +$52   |
| After 11 AM    | -$132  |

Every trade I took that afternoon was a loser.

I went from having a profitable morning to giving back more than I had made because I wasn't satisfied with the original result.

## When profit doesn't feel like enough

I think part of the problem is how I measure a successful trading day.

I get so focused on what I want to make that I start treating anything less than that number like an unfinished job.

If the goal is $100, then making $52 somehow feels like falling short.

But that's backwards.

The market doesn't owe me $100 because I showed up that morning. There's no reason I should expect a certain amount of money just because I spent a few hours watching charts.

And the more I try to force that number, the more likely I am to take trades I probably wouldn't have taken otherwise.

## The part I'm trying to understand

I've had other days where I made decent money and still kept trading.

It's almost like I can't leave the money alone once it's there.

Instead of protecting what I've already made, I'm thinking about how much more I could make.

Then one losing trade turns into another, and suddenly I'm trying to recover money that I didn't even need to risk in the first place.

I don't think having a financial goal is necessarily the problem. It's what happens when I start forcing trades to meet it.

## Something to work on

I want to get away from treating a daily dollar amount as the definition of a good trading day.

If I find a good setup and execute it properly, that's enough. If nothing is there, there's nothing to force.

Making $52 doesn't become a bad day just because I wanted $100.

I haven't completely worked this out yet, but November 9 gave me a pretty clear example of what happens when I let a number dictate my decisions.

Sometimes the money I'm trying to make is the same money I end up losing trying to make more.
