# The Give-Back

I've been going back through my trading history, and honestly, it's a little ridiculous when you put everything together.

The pattern is almost always the same. Deposit $10 or $15, start trading, build the account up, get excited because things are finally working, and then somehow lose almost everything.

Then I deposit again.

And again.

I've counted **52 separate times** where my account went from a small deposit to at least $10 and eventually dropped back under $1.

Fifty-two times.

I wanted to see just how bad it actually was, so I went through my MT5 and Webull records. Here's what I found.

## Where the money went

On MT5 (Coinexx), between May 2025 and November 2026, I made 59 deposits.

Almost all of them were between $10 and $17.

- Total deposited: **$752.10**
- Total withdrawals: **$0**
- Current balance: **$0.49**

That's right. I haven't withdrawn anything.

I kept putting money back in, thinking I could start small, flip the account, and eventually keep what I made.

Some of the deposits:

- May 2025: Two deposits of $17.32 and $17.10.
- June 2025: Five deposits, all around $16–$17.
- April–May 2026: Eight deposits in one month, all around $10.50.
- November 3–6, 2026: Ten deposits in four days.

That last stretch alone was about $128 just trying to keep the account going.

Looking at it now, I was basically paying for another opportunity to repeat the same cycle.

This is probably the most frustrating part.

It's not like I never made money. I've had some pretty impressive runs starting with very little.

That September 2025 run still stands out.

I started with $17.67. By September 8, the account had reached $209.84.

For a minute, I was actually doing it.

But here's how quickly things changed:

- August 19: $5.90

By the end of September 8, the balance was down to $20.26.

Three days later, it was $0.18.

**The biggest trading win of my life was practically gone the same day.**

And apparently, I didn't learn my lesson because November gave me another example.

On November 3, I took $11.10 to $140.55, then ended the day with $0.53.

The next day, I deposited again. Took $15.79 to $80.28, and by November 5, the account was at negative $0.28.

I don't even know what else to say about that.

## So what am I doing wrong?

After looking through the individual trades, a few things became pretty obvious.

**I hold onto winners too long.**

I'll be sitting on a decent profit, watch the market reverse, and somehow turn a winning trade into a loser.

On November 9, I bought a MU 765 call at $0.73 and held it until I sold at $0.04.

That's a $69 loss on a 0DTE contract I held for almost two hours.

I knew I was in trouble, but I kept holding.

**I increase my position size after winning.**

Silver is a good example. I went from 0.01 lots to 0.05 lots during the same session.

Things were working, so I started taking on more risk.

Then four losing trades wiped out what I'd built.

**I keep chasing a dollar amount.**

This might be the biggest one.

On November 9, I was up $52 by 10:48 AM across three trades.

Instead of being satisfied with that, I kept going because I wanted to make $100.

I even said it myself:

> "i got in that mu call at the wrong time and im ngl i just gambled that because i wanted to make atleast $100 today"

That's exactly what happened.

I had a profitable morning and turned it into a losing day because I wasn't satisfied with what I'd already made.

From 9:42 to 10:48 AM, I made $52.

Everything after 11 AM came out to -$132.

Every afternoon trade that day was a loser.

I keep thinking one more trade might help me make back what I lost.

> "maybe one of these wins will help me make it all back + more"

But that's the problem. The wins have already happened.

I've had 52 account runs where I managed to build something. Even that $209.84 run wasn't enough to recover what I'd deposited over time.

I'm so focused on making the next big win that I haven't figured out how to keep the money I've already made.

## What I'm going to test

One thing I noticed is that most of my entries happen between 9 and 10 AM. My median holding time is around 11 minutes.

That's also where I've generally had my better trading activity.

So I'm considering a pretty simple rule:

**Stop trading before lunch.**

Not because some trading guru said that's what I'm supposed to do. Not because there's some magic in the clock.

I'm looking at my own records, and the afternoon trading is where I've repeatedly gotten myself into trouble.

I also need to stop increasing my size just because I'm having a good morning. And I need to get away from these daily dollar targets.

Making $100 shouldn't be the requirement for having a successful trading day.

But I'm not going to pretend I've fixed anything yet. This is a rule I want to test, not a conclusion I've already proven.

## Where I stand

I honestly don't know if I want to keep doing this.

I've spent a lot of time learning about ICT, liquidity, order blocks, fair value gaps, opening range breakouts, and all kinds of other market mechanics.

I've spent money, time, and countless hours staring at charts.

And sometimes I sit back and wonder what the hell I've been doing all this for.

I thought I was getting better. I thought I was getting closer.

Then I look at the deposits, the withdrawals, and the actual results.

It's humbling.

I don't want to keep depositing money just to see if I can flip another small account. I've already demonstrated that I can grow one. That's not the part I need to prove anymore.

The question is whether I can trade without giving everything back.

For now, I'm documenting what happened. I'm not promising a comeback, and I'm not declaring trading dead either.

I just want to be honest about where I actually stand.
