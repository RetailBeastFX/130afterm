## The pattern

Start with a small amount. Flip it. Lose it all. Repeat.

MT5 (Coinexx, account 973451, May 2025 – Nov 2026): 59 deposits, almost all $10–$17 each, $752.10 in total. Zero withdrawals, ever. Balance now: $0.49.

Fifty-two times the account ran from a small deposit to a peak of $10 or more — and back under $1. Every single one.

**How a cycle is defined:** a cycle starts when the balance is under $1 — a fresh deposit into an empty account — and it counts once the balance climbs to $10 or more and then returns below $1. Fifty-two cycles met that bar. Smaller top-ups that never reached $10 aren't in that count; they lost too, just without a peak. The table below shows the eight cycles that peaked above $50.

**The three headline numbers reconcile exactly:** $752.10 deposited − $751.61 net loss = $0.49 balance. No commissions were charged on any deal, and swaps total −$8.16 across the whole history — so the loss is trading losses, not fees.

> "i think im ready to quit trading. I thought i was good at this i may be mentally insane."

Whether I keep trading is an open question. This note is the evidence for it, either way.

The idea was always the same: start small, flip it, and this time keep it.

## What the numbers actually say

The sample is big enough to call expectancy negative. That's real, not noise.

Winning trades show I can find moves that pay — the good days are real: 4-for-4 puts for +$57 on Nov 5, a +$58 IWM win Nov 6, +$52 by 10:48 AM on Nov 9. But that doesn't establish a directional edge on its own. A 40.5% win rate with an average win barely above the average loss still leaves expectancy negative. What the record proves is narrower: I can get into profitable trades. I can't yet keep the profits.

## What actually kills it

- **Winners get held until they reverse.** Nov 5, silver ran to $36, then four losses at 0.05 lots (-$9.50 / -$9.00 / -$12.50 / -$10.35) wiped it in about 30 minutes. Nov 9, the MU 765 call was bought at 11:08 for $0.73 and sold at 12:59 for $0.04 — -$69, a 0DTE call held two hours.
- **Size scales up after wins.** Silver went 0.01 → 0.05 lots inside the same session that was working.
- **A daily dollar target forces trades.** Nov 9 I was +$52 by 10:48. The target was $100, so +$52 read as "not enough yet."

> "i got in that mu call at the wrong time and im ngl i just gambled that because i wanted to make atleast $100 today"

Nov 9 splits cleanly: morning (9:42–10:48 AM) +$52 across 3 trades; everything after 11 AM, -$132. Every afternoon trade that day was a loser.

> "maybe one of these wins will help me make it all back + more"

The wins already happened — fifty-two of them. Even the biggest, $209.84, is less than a third of the $752.10 that's gone in. The win isn't the missing piece. Keeping one is.

## The rule I'm testing

> "I just shouldnt have over traded"
> "and stopped before lunch"

Don't overtrade. Stop before lunch. My entries have always clustered 9–10 AM with a median hold around 11 minutes — the rule matches the window where I actually do well. It isn't tested yet. That's what this note is for: the rule is stated, the evidence for it is Nov 9, and the next sessions decide whether it holds.
