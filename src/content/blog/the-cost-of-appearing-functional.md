**Some people ain’t incapable. They’re just tired of having to prove they can function every single day.**

I’ve been thinking about how people define a “functional adult,” especially when it comes to work.

Apparently you’re supposed to wake up on time every day, show up on schedule, stay productive, deal with a boss watching you, talk to people the “right” way, keep your emotions in check, and do all that forever without slipping.

And if you struggle with any of it, folks start talking about your character.

You late? You irresponsible.  
You inconsistent? You lazy.  
You don’t really socialize? You antisocial.  
You don’t like being micromanaged? You difficult.  
You need some accommodations? You must not be capable enough.

But what if the problem ain’t that you can’t do the work? What if the problem is the conditions they put around the work?

There’s a difference between being able to do something and being able to do it on somebody else’s schedule, in their environment, under their rules, every single day.

Somebody can be smart, skilled, creative, and good at solving real problems, and still struggle with focus, sensory overload, dealing with people all day, or keeping their emotions steady.

For some people, ADHD, autism, or CPTSD can make regular job expectations way harder than they look from the outside.

And that’s before you even count the energy it takes just to look like you got it together.

Some people spend the whole day covering stuff up. Masking how uncomfortable they are, forcing conversations, holding in reactions, fighting distractions, managing anxiety, acting like it’s natural. Then they go home drained as hell and have to do it all over again the next day.

The wild part is, if they hide it well enough, everybody thinks they straight.

Until they can’t keep it up no more.

Then all of a sudden, they're “unreliable.”

Look, accountability still matters. If you miss commitments, it affects other people. Responsibilities don’t just disappear because somebody got a different nervous system. But we can hold people accountable without turning every struggle into a character flaw.

What bothers me is how quick society confuses being able to contribute with being able to put up with the whole process of contributing.

Maybe somebody don’t need to be watched every five minutes. Maybe they work better alone. Maybe they need flexible hours, less interruptions, written instructions, or a space where they ain’t constantly being judged.

Why do those differences automatically mean they're less capable?

And why is being able to tolerate a regular 9-to-5 treated like the main measure of somebody’s potential?

Not everybody is built for that traditional setup. That don’t mean everybody need to go start a business either. People just need real options and real support. Ways to make a living that don’t require them to break themselves just to stay employed.

I think we sleep on how much potential gets buried under expectations that ain’t even about the actual work.

Some people ain’t lacking ambition. They just fighting battles that don’t show up on a résumé.

Maybe the question shouldn’t always be “why can’t certain people function normal?”

Maybe we also need to ask why “functioning normal” got such a narrow definition in the first place?

**Surviving an environment ain’t the same as thriving in it.**
