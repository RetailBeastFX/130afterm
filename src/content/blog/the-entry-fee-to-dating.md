I came across a tweet the other day that got me thinking about the expectations surrounding dating.

The conversation was about men questioning the traditional dating script and why some women seem confused when men express that they no longer find the arrangement particularly appealing.

One response suggested something interesting: propose meeting at a park instead of taking somebody out for an elaborate date and watch how quickly the energy changes.

The park wasn't really the point. The idea was to suggest something simple, inexpensive, and relaxed where two people could sit down, talk, and get to know each other without turning the occasion into a production.

For some, that suggestion appears to diminish the appeal of the occasion.

That got me thinking about something bigger than whether a park is an acceptable first date.

Why has dating become so closely associated with what a man is willing to spend, how elaborate an experience he can arrange, and how well he can perform the role of someone worth pursuing?

I'm not against taking somebody out, spending money, planning something thoughtful, or making a woman feel special. Those things can be genuine expressions of interest.

But there's a difference between wanting to do something for somebody because you like them and feeling like you have to do it just to qualify for their consideration.

I think a lot of the frustration surrounding modern dating comes down to that distinction.
