When I was younger, I was a whole hopeless romantic.

I used to catch feelings hard for girls and get locked into limerence, building entire fantasies around women who barely even knew my name. I really believed in romance. I thought love was something special, something worth chasing, and that if I had a good heart and pure intentions, eventually a woman would see that and choose me.

I knew I wasn’t no ugly dude. I knew I had something to offer. I genuinely thought a girl would be lucky to have me.

I was green as hell.

I had to learn the hard way that just ’cause you sincerely want to love somebody don’t mean they gon’ want you back. I dealt with rejection, humiliation, and situations that made me feel like I was nothing. I spent too much time caring whether certain women saw any value in me, giving people power over how I felt about myself.

Eventually, I got tired of that shit.

I didn’t want to be that dude no more. I wanted to stop putting women on pedestals, stop seeking their approval, and stop treating their attention like it said something about my worth.

I wanted to like myself the same way I used to like them girls. I wanted to be the one doing the choosing instead of always hoping somebody would choose me. I wanted to get to a place where rejection couldn’t shake me and women’s attention wasn’t something I needed just to feel good about myself.

And somewhere along the way, I stopped believing in the whole romantic ideal.

The more I learned about attraction, social hierarchies, hypergamy, and how people actually pick who they fuck with, the harder it got to match what I was seeing with what they taught us about love.

Then you got all the shit you hear everywhere. Conversations, music, stories, women out here openly saying the type of men they want and what really attracts them. Sometimes the preferences they were describing felt completely opposite from the qualities I thought people were supposed to value in a partner.

I ain’t saying every woman the same. I’m saying I seen enough examples, in enough different settings, that I started questioning the whole picture of romance I been carrying in my head.

I used to think having a good heart and genuine intentions counted for more than they apparently did in the situations I went through.

Eventually, I started wondering if I just bought into a fantasy.

We grow up surrounded by stories about love. Find your person. Meet somebody who really sees you. Fall in love, build a life together, live happily ever after.

But nobody can promise you that.

Attraction ain’t no reward for being a good person. Being desired don’t automatically mean being valued. And being in a relationship don’t mean you found something worth having.

People got preferences, conditions, competing interests, and feelings that change. They can love you and still disappoint you. They can make you feel special without seeing the connection the same way you do. And sometimes two people walk into a relationship with completely different ideas about what they building.

I started looking at romance different. What I once thought was this beautiful, almost sacred thing started looking like something people idealized way past what human beings can actually deliver.

It’s kind of like finding out Santa Claus ain’t real. Once you see what’s behind the story, you can’t go back to believing the same way.

And now, when I watch romance movies, I can’t even look at them the same way.

That shit looks like propaganda to me sometimes.

The same fantasy gets repackaged over and over again. Two people meet, fall in love, overcome some obstacle, and eventually end up together. Roll the credits. Everybody’s happy. That’s the dream we’re sold.

And growing up, I bought into it. I didn’t question why romance was presented as something everybody should want, why being single was so often portrayed as a problem to solve, or why finding a partner was treated like some kind of final destination in life.

Now I look at it differently.

Romance leads into marriage. Marriage often leads into children. And children grow up to become the next generation of workers, consumers, and taxpayers. Society depends on new generations to keep its institutions and economy going, so of course there’s a cultural interest in keeping certain traditions alive.

I’m not saying every love story is part of some secret government plot. I’m saying the ideal serves a purpose beyond making people feel good.

When an entire culture keeps telling you that romantic love is one of the greatest things you can experience, you can spend years chasing it without ever asking yourself whether it’s something you genuinely want or something you’ve been taught to want.

And that’s the part that gets me.

How much of what we call our dreams actually came from us? How much did we inherit from movies, music, family, religion, and a society that already had a picture of what a successful life was supposed to look like?

I spent years thinking I was missing something because I hadn’t found the love I’d been promised. Now I’m questioning the promise itself.

Maybe the real freedom is being able to look at the life everybody keeps advertising to you and decide you don’t want to buy it.

And the wild part is I really believed in it. I wasn’t faking being a romantic. I genuinely wanted that kind of love when I was younger.

Looking back, I don’t think that version of me was stupid for wanting it. I think part of me already knew things weren’t as simple as the stories made them seem. I saw things that didn’t quite add up, but I still believed there was something about me that would make things work out differently.

I knew I was quiet, but I still thought I was cool enough. I knew I wasn’t everybody’s type, but I figured the right woman would see what I had to offer. I believed that even if things didn’t work out for everybody else, maybe they could work out for me.

I wasn’t completely blind to reality. I just didn’t think I’d have to experience certain things for myself.

And eventually, I did.

The experiences I went through didn’t necessarily introduce me to a reality I’d never considered. They confirmed things I had suspected but wasn’t ready to fully accept. It’s one thing to recognize something from a distance. It’s another thing to live through it and realize you can’t keep making yourself the exception.

My own relationship only complicated them feelings. I been through things that made me question whether being in a relationship actually give the peace, trust, and companionship people claim it do. At some point, I had to admit the reality of my experience wasn’t matching the ideal I once believed in.

And then there’s the realization that I might be neurodivergent. I’m still trying to understand myself, but it made me look back at my life different. Sometimes it feel like other people just know how to move through social situations and relationships naturally, while I spent years trying to figure out a world that never quite made sense to me.

I don’t just question whether romance is worth pursuing anymore. Sometimes I question whether I’ll ever meet somebody who really understand me.

And I think the biggest thing I had to survive wasn’t rejection itself. It was what rejection made me believe about myself.

I spent years trying to understand what I was doing wrong, what women wanted, and what I needed to change about myself to become somebody they would choose. I was trying to figure out the formula. If I could just understand attraction, become more desirable, and get to the point where rejection didn’t happen anymore, maybe I’d finally get what I wanted.

I didn’t realize how much of myself I was putting on the line in that process.

I was turning something I wanted into something I needed to prove I could get. Every rejection became another reason to examine myself. Every disappointment gave me something new to question. Instead of simply accepting that somebody wasn’t interested, I’d find myself trying to understand what that meant about me.

Eventually, I got tired of trying to solve myself for other people’s approval.

I don’t want to spend the rest of my life becoming somebody’s ideal man. I don’t want to study what makes me desirable just so I can finally feel like I’m enough. I don’t want to keep measuring myself against what other people want and adjusting my life around the possibility of being chosen.

I want to be able to exist without feeling like I’m trying out for a role.

And I think that’s what changed the most. I stopped looking at myself as somebody who needed to become worthy of being wanted. I stopped treating romantic attention like the final confirmation that I had something valuable to offer.

I wanted love, but I didn’t want to lose myself trying to get it.

And somewhere along the way, I finally stopped.

But I also accepted something about myself: I don’t want to spend my life chasing romance just ’cause that’s what people expect you to want.

I don’t want to compete with other men for a woman’s attention. I don’t want to prove I’m the best option. I don’t want to build my life around trying to earn somebody’s affection or gamble my peace on the hope that another person gon’ treat me the way I hope they will.

And I don’t want to pretend I still believe in something I done grew out of.

The truth is, the part of me that wanted romantic love has changed. What I still fuck with is sexual attraction, not the idea of building my whole life around a romantic relationship. If I was free to choose without the complications I got right now, I’d choose to stay single.

Not ’cause I’m waiting on somebody better. Not ’cause I’m trying to make women regret overlooking me. And not ’cause I need to prove I can live without them.

I simply don’t want romance no more.

Maybe some people never gon’ understand that. They gon’ assume I’m bitter, hurt, scared of commitment, or secretly waiting for the right woman to change my mind. People get comfortable believing everybody wants the same things they do.

But I done spent enough of my life trying to understand what everybody else seems to want. I don’t feel obligated to keep chasing it.

I’m not looking for somebody to restore my faith in romance. I’m not waiting on a woman to prove everything I’ve come to believe is wrong.

I wanted romance once. I believed in it. I chased it. And somewhere along the way, I realized I was putting too much of myself into trying to obtain something I no longer even wanted for my life.

I feel like I survived that version of myself.

Not because wanting love was a weakness, but because I finally stopped making being loved the answer to everything I thought was missing.

I don’t need to turn romance into the enemy. I don’t need to convince everybody else that they’re wrong for wanting it. And I don’t need to pretend I never wanted it myself.

I just don’t want it anymore.

I’d rather build a life that makes sense to me than spend the rest of it chasing an ideal I don’t believe in no more.

I spent years trying to become somebody worth choosing. Now I just want to live without feeling like I have to be chosen at all.
