*When surviving the struggle becomes a status symbol.*

One thing I've noticed is how people have been conditioned to treat suffering like some kind of achievement.

You survived poverty? You're a hustler. You grew up without much? That made you tougher. You endured mistreatment, instability, and years of unnecessary hardship? Now you're supposedly built different.

And somewhere along the way, surviving all that becomes a measuring stick for who's real, who's soft, and who deserves respect.

On God, they got people thinking surviving the struggle makes you realer than the next man.

But surviving something doesn't automatically make you superior to somebody who didn't. You didn't unlock some higher level of humanity because you learned to tolerate more pain. You adapted to circumstances that shouldn't have been normalized in the first place.

That's what bothers me about the glorification of suffering. It can turn systemic failures into personal accomplishments. Instead of questioning why people have to struggle so hard just to maintain a basic standard of living, we celebrate how well they've learned to function under those conditions.

And the people who can't keep up? Somehow, they're lazy. They lack discipline. They don't want it badly enough.

Meanwhile, the conditions remain untouched.

There's a difference between respecting somebody's resilience and romanticizing what they had to endure. I can respect the person who made something out of very little without pretending that having very little was some necessary character-building experience.

You can appreciate your own journey without turning your hardship into somebody else's standard.

Because what are we really celebrating at that point? Progress, or how much mistreatment people have learned to accept?

**Being able to survive a broken situation doesn't mean you've overcome it. And it damn sure doesn't mean the situation deserves to stay broken.**
