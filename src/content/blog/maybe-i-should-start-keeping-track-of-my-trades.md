**I can tell you how much money I've lost trading, but I can't always tell you exactly where I'm going wrong. And that's a problem.**

I'll be honest. I don't journal my trades.

I've heard all the usual advice about keeping a trading journal. Record your entries, exits, screenshots, emotions, wins, losses, all that.

And I understand why people recommend it.

But actually sitting down and doing it consistently? That's a different story.

Most of the time, I'm focused on finding trades, watching the charts and figuring out what I could've done differently after something happens.

Sometimes I remember exactly why I entered a position. Other times, I just remember the result.

And that's not necessarily enough to figure out what's going on.

Think about it.

You might remember that one trade where you made $200, but forget all the smaller losses that happened trying to get there.

You might feel like a particular strategy works because you've seen it produce some good trades.

But how many times have you actually used it?

How many times did it fail?

Are you losing because the strategy isn't good, or because you're not following it properly?

Those are two completely different problems.

**Sometimes I think traders spend more time looking for another strategy than figuring out what's wrong with the ones they already have.**

I'm guilty of experimenting with different indicators, setups and timeframes. There's always something else to try.

But at some point, you have to wonder whether you're actually improving your approach or just looking for something new every time the current approach stops working.

That's where keeping track of your trades could come in handy.

Not because writing everything down is going to magically make you profitable, but because you might start noticing things you wouldn't catch just going off memory.

Maybe you do better trading certain stocks.

Maybe you're more successful during the first hour of the market.

Maybe you're making money on puts but giving it back on calls.

Maybe your biggest losses happen when you keep trading after already having a good start to the day.

I honestly don't know what my own records would reveal if I consistently tracked all of that.

And that's kind of the point.

I shouldn't have to guess.

I want to get to a point where I can look at actual numbers instead of relying on how I feel about my trading.

Because it's easy to convince yourself you're getting better when you remember the wins and overlook everything else.

I'm not going to sit here and preach about discipline and journaling like I've mastered either one.

I haven't.

But I do think there's a difference between wanting to become a better trader and actually doing the things that help you figure out whether you're improving.

Maybe keeping better records is one of those things I need to start taking more seriously.

At the very least, I'd rather know where my money is going than keep making the same mistakes without understanding why.
