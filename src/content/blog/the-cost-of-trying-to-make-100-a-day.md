**Sometimes you end up losing money because you came into the market already expecting to make money.**

I've caught myself doing this more times than I'd like to admit.

You open your trading platform with a number in your head. Maybe you want to make $50. Maybe $100. Whatever the number is, you already decided what a successful day is supposed to look like before the market even gets moving.

Now you're sitting there watching charts, waiting for something to happen.

But what if nothing looks good?

Instead of leaving it alone, you start looking harder. Maybe you switch to another stock. Maybe you jump into options. Maybe you start watching another timeframe looking for an opportunity.

Next thing you know, you're taking trades you probably would've passed on if you weren't so focused on making money.

That's where the trouble starts.

**The market doesn't know you need $100 today.**

It doesn't know you got bills. It doesn't know you lost money yesterday. It doesn't care that you woke up ready to make something happen.

Price is going to do whatever it's going to do.

And sometimes that's the hardest part about trading. You can sit there for hours and not get the kind of opportunity you're looking for.

I've had days where I started off green, gave some of it back, and kept trading because I wanted to reach a certain number.

That's how you can turn a decent day into a bad one.

You already made money, but instead of being satisfied with what the market gave you, you're trying to squeeze more out of it.

And now you're risking money you didn't have to risk in the first place.

I think a lot of traders get caught up in setting daily income goals without really thinking about what it takes to reach them.

Making $100 a day sounds good. That's $500 a week. That's real money.

But there's a difference between having a goal of eventually making $100 a day and feeling like you HAVE to make $100 every time you open your platform.

One is something you're working toward. The other can have you forcing trades just to feel like you accomplished something.

**Sometimes not trading is the smartest move you can make.**

I know that probably sounds obvious, but it's easier said than done when you're sitting there watching the market move without you.

You see something running and start thinking about how much you could've made. You start feeling like you're missing out.

Then you jump into the next opportunity without really knowing whether it's worth taking.

That's how chasing money turns into chasing losses.

I'm not saying don't have financial goals. Obviously, most of us aren't trading just for entertainment. We want to make money.

But I'd rather get to a point where my trading decisions are based on actual opportunities instead of how much money I feel like I should've made by the end of the day.

Because trying to force $100 out of the market can easily turn into losing $100 trying to get it.

And I've already learned that lesson the expensive way.
