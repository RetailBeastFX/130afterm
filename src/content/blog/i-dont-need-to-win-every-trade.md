**Everybody wants to win, but winning more trades doesn't automatically mean you're making more money.**

One thing I've noticed about trading is how much people focus on win rates.

You'll see somebody talking about a strategy with an 80% win rate, and everybody gets excited.

But what does that actually mean if the losses are bigger than the wins?

You could win eight trades out of ten and still end up losing money.

Meanwhile, somebody else could lose six out of ten trades and still come out profitable.

It all depends on how much they're making when they're right versus how much they're losing when they're wrong.

Let me put it in simple terms.

Say you make $20 on seven trades but lose $60 on the other three.

You made $140, but lost $180.

You're down $40 even though you won most of your trades.

Now imagine somebody who only wins four out of ten trades.

They make $100 on each winner and lose $40 on each loser.

That's $400 in profit and $240 in losses.

They're up $160.

Who would you rather be?

That's why I don't think win rate tells the whole story.

**Being right a lot and actually being profitable are two different things.**

I think it's easy to get caught up in wanting every trade to go your way. Nobody likes taking losses, especially when you feel like you read the chart correctly.

But losses are part of the game.

The bigger question is whether your approach actually makes money over time.

I've spent a lot of time experimenting with different strategies, indicators, timeframes and setups. Sometimes you find something that looks promising, but that doesn't necessarily mean it's going to work consistently.

And that's something I'm still figuring out myself.

Just because an entry looks good doesn't mean there's money in the strategy.

Just because somebody online posted a winning trade doesn't mean they found some secret formula.

At some point, you have to stop looking at individual wins and losses and start looking at the bigger picture.

How often does the setup work? How much does it make when it works? How much does it lose when it doesn't?

And does it still make sense after fees, spreads and bad executions?

Those are the things that actually matter.

I'm not interested in finding some strategy that makes me feel like I'm right all the time.

I'd rather find something I can understand, test and potentially make money with consistently.

Because the goal isn't to win every trade.

The goal is to stop confusing winning trades with a winning strategy.
