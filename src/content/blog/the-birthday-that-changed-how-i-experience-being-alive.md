There's something uncomfortable about being reminded that you're alive.

Not in some philosophical, appreciate-the-little-things kind of way. I mean physically. Sometimes I'll lay down to go to sleep, become overly aware of my breathing, notice how dry my mouth feels, and suddenly find myself thinking about suffocating.

It's strange how quickly an ordinary moment can turn into an existential crisis.

I didn't always experience things this way. As far as I can remember, these feelings started after my 30th birthday.

## August 17, 2025

I turned 30 on August 17, 2025. What should've been a regular birthday ended up being one of the more frightening physical experiences I've ever had.

I'd been drinking, smoking, and celebrating. I also had to work an overnight shift at Amazon, starting at 1:20 in the morning. I ended up leaving early because I felt physically exhausted and unwell.

I went home, slept for roughly three hours, and woke up feeling completely out of sorts.

My head felt strange, almost like electrical sensations were running through it. My scalp felt incredibly hot. I was having trouble breathing and felt like my body wasn't handling it automatically anymore. I couldn't get comfortable, couldn't sit still, and couldn't even lay down without feeling like something was seriously wrong.

I spent the next couple of hours pacing around, trying to get some relief. For whatever reason, forcing myself to burp seemed to relieve some of the pressure I was feeling.

I eventually recovered at home, but the experience stuck with me.

I never really got a medical explanation for what happened, either.

## When Your Body Becomes Something You Monitor

What's interesting is that I didn't necessarily become afraid of death overnight. I became more conscious of my body's vulnerability.

Before that experience, breathing was just breathing. I didn't spend time thinking about whether I was getting enough air or whether my body was doing what it was supposed to do.

Now, every once in a while, I'll notice something as simple as a dry throat or an uncomfortable sensation when inhaling, and my mind starts connecting dots that probably don't need connecting.

Am I dehydrated? Is something wrong with my breathing? Why does the air feel like this?

Sometimes I become so conscious of the process of breathing that it starts feeling unnatural.

And that's when the fear can take over.

It's not necessarily that I believe I'm dying in that moment. It's that the sensation reminds me that I could die. That my body is something I depend on but don't have complete control over.

I can usually distract myself and move on, but those moments can be genuinely terrifying.

## I Used to Look for Something Beyond the Physical

There's another interesting contrast when I think about how I viewed existence growing up.

As a teenager, I was fascinated by things like astral projection, soul traveling, and the possibility of consciousness existing outside the physical body.

I wanted to experience something that would prove there was more to existence than what we could physically observe.

I was genuinely excited by the possibility, but I also wanted evidence.

Eventually, I stopped believing in those explanations. I became less interested in accepting what people told me and more interested in what I could actually establish through experience.

I still don't have an answer to the questions I was exploring back then.

I don't know whether consciousness continues after death. I don't know whether there's something beyond our physical existence. And I'm not particularly interested in pretending I know.

But there's a difference between being curious about those questions and being terrified by them.

I used to explore the possibility of something beyond my body because it fascinated me.

Now, sometimes, I'm just trying to get comfortable enough to fall asleep without being reminded that my existence depends on something as seemingly simple as breathing.

## The Uncomfortable Part of Being Alive

I think what bothers me most is the combination of things I can't control.

The possibility of physical suffering. The fact that existence eventually ends. The uncertainty surrounding what happens afterward. And the helplessness of knowing that some things are simply outside my control.

It's one thing to understand those concepts intellectually. It's another thing to experience a physical sensation that makes them feel immediate.

Sometimes I'll be lying in bed, perfectly aware that I'm probably just uncomfortable, and somehow my thoughts end up somewhere much darker.

I don't necessarily want to die. Quite the opposite. I want to live, experience things, accomplish things, and enjoy the time I have.

But occasionally, the awareness that all of this is temporary becomes so overwhelming that simply existing feels uncomfortable.

I don't have some profound conclusion about mortality. I haven't discovered a spiritual explanation that makes everything easier, and I don't think pretending to have one would accomplish much.

I just know that something happened on my 30th birthday that changed how I sometimes experience my own existence.

And I'm still trying to make sense of it.
