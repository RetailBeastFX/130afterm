Something I think about is how we’re taught to make money, but not necessarily what to do with it once we have it.

Go to school. Get a degree. Find a job. Work your way up. Make more money.

But making money, building wealth, and gaining control over your time are three different things.

You can earn a good salary and still be dependent on your next paycheck. You can work for years, increase your income, and never build enough assets to give yourself more options.

That’s part of why I look at trading the way I do. It’s not just about making money off the market. If you can become consistently profitable, you have the opportunity to put some of those profits toward long-term investments instead of keeping everything tied to your next trade.

Dividend-paying ETFs are one example. So are other investments and assets that can grow in value or generate income over time.

Obviously, trading isn’t guaranteed income, investments carry risk, and dividends aren’t guaranteed either. I’m not saying everybody should become a trader.

I’m talking about the bigger picture.

Why is so much of the conversation about getting a job and earning a paycheck, but so little of it about capital, ownership, compounding, and building something that makes you less dependent on your next paycheck?

Even trading can become another trap if your entire livelihood depends on making money every day. The market doesn’t care if your rent is due. You’ve potentially replaced one source of pressure with another.

The point isn’t to escape one system just to become completely dependent on another.

It’s to use whatever income you can generate to build a position where you have more choices. Where one bad month doesn’t dictate your life. Where taking time off doesn’t automatically mean losing your ability to pay your bills.

The goal isn’t just to get paid. It’s to reach a point where getting paid today isn’t the only thing standing between you and the life you want.
