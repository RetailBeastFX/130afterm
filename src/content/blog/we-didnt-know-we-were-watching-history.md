**We didn't have to go looking for the culture that came before us. It was already part of the culture we grew up with.**

I saw a conversation about millennials having a different level of cultural literacy because of the movies, cartoons, music, and television we grew up watching. One reply in particular caught my attention.

Somebody pointed out that classic Looney Tunes would play right after whatever modern cartoon was on. They were born in '92 and didn't even realize those cartoons, movies, and stories were sometimes older than their parents.

I felt that.

Because that's really how it was. We weren't sitting around looking for vintage entertainment. We weren't researching the history of animation or trying to educate ourselves on classic Hollywood.

We were just watching TV.

Bugs Bunny was Bugs Bunny. Tom and Jerry was Tom and Jerry. You'd watch whatever cartoon was on, and then an older cartoon would come on right after it. Nobody stopped to explain that you were watching something made decades before you were born.

The same thing happened with music, movies, and television shows. We were exposed to entertainment from different generations without really thinking about it.

And that's what I find interesting.

We didn't necessarily appreciate the history behind everything we watched. We just became familiar with it. The appreciation sometimes came later, when we got older and realized how much of what we enjoyed was actually inherited from generations before us.

Think about how many references we picked up without even knowing where they came from.

Cartoons introduced us to classical music, opera, old Hollywood, and literature. Shows like The Simpsons were constantly referencing movies, celebrities, music, and events that happened long before we were born.

You could laugh at something without understanding the reference. Then years later, you'd encounter the original material and realize where it came from.

That's a pretty interesting way to learn about culture without even realizing you're learning anything.

I also think the way we consumed entertainment played a big part in it.

A lot of us grew up with one television in the house. You didn't always have control over what was playing. Sometimes you watched whatever everybody else was watching. Sometimes you watched something because there wasn't anything else on.

And when you had fewer options, you were more likely to stumble across things you wouldn't have intentionally searched for.

Today, you can have five people in one household watching five completely different things. Everybody has their own screen, their own recommendations, and their own little corner of the internet.

There's nothing inherently wrong with having more choices. I actually enjoy having access to things that previous generations couldn't easily find.

But there's a difference between having access to everything and being exposed to a little bit of everything.

I don't think this means millennials are automatically more cultured or that younger generations are somehow missing something intellectually. That would be taking the conversation too far.

I think we experienced a different kind of cultural environment.

We grew up with entertainment that didn't always separate generations. Something made in the 1940s could sit right next to something made in the 1990s, and to a kid watching television, it was all just entertainment.

Maybe that's something we've lost a little of.

Not the ability to access older culture, but the natural exposure to it.

We didn't know we were watching history. We were just enjoying ourselves.

And now that we're older, we can look back and realize just how much of the past was quietly becoming part of our present.

**Sometimes you don't realize what you inherited until you get old enough to recognize where it came from.**
